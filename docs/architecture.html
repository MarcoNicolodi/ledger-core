Ledger core — architecture, trade-offs, production

Pure Clojure core: one immutable state map, one append-only journal vector. Every balance, hold and authorization state is derived from the journal. Each record has a posted-day and a value-date, so any past view ("as known at Day N") is a filter, not a copy.

1. Append-only at scale

What breaks first at 100×: the end-of-day run. Every query scans the whole journal (O(n)), and the fee run calls balance for every day from Day 1 to today, for every account. Cost per close is accounts × days × journal size, so 100× accounts and 100× events gives about 10,000× the work. auth-state scans the journal of all accounts, not one.

Unbounded state: (1) the journal itself, never compacted; (2) the fee look-back starts at Day 1 forever, so its range grows with every day; (3) one :accrual record per account per day; (4) :known-at views need all history in memory.

Cheapest structural change: partition the journal by account ({account-id [records]}). All reads already go through journal/records, so the change is local. Queries become O(records of one account), accounts can be processed in parallel and later sharded. Next step: a closed-day checkpoint record (closing balance by value date) plus a back-valuation horizon, so the look-back stops at the last checkpoint instead of Day 1.

2. Value-dated entries in production (UAE-licensed bank)

  • Restated history. A back-dated entry changes balances that the customer already saw and that fees and interest already used. Statements need adjustment lines and customer notice.
  • Fees and consumer protection. CBUAE consumer-protection rules expect fees to be disclosed and fair; a fee caused by the bank's own wrong debit (E7) is a complaint and remediation case. VAT at 5% applies to explicit fees, and a back-dated fee can fall into a closed VAT period.
  • Regulatory reporting and accounting. CBUAE returns and the general ledger are point-in-time. Back-dated entries must post in the open period (booking date) while interest uses the value date.
  • Fraud and AML. Back-dating is a classic manipulation path (hide movements, avoid fees). It needs a full audit trail and monitoring under the UAE AML/CFT framework.
  • Islamic products. For Sharia-compliant accounts, "interest" is profit under a contract; value dating changes the profit calculation and needs Sharia-board-approved rules.

Control before going live: a back-valuation gate. Any entry whose value date is earlier than the previous business day needs a reason code and maker-checker approval, cannot fall into a locked period, and automatically produces a report of the fees and interest it changes.

3. Authorization lifecycle

In this model an authorization has only two terminal states: settled and declined. Every other path leaves it open or never creates it.
